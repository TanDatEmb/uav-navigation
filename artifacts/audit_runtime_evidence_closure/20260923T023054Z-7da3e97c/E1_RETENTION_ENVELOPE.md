# E1 — retention evidence, not a chosen timeout

`MEASURED_ENVELOPE`: one TARGET episode has first in-ball observation at 95.732 s and first published true continuation at 96.971999999 s, a 1.240 s source-time delay. The vehicle left the ball after 96.372 s and re-entered at 97.912 s; current product accepted at 97.916 s. This observation establishes scheduling/readiness separation, **not** that retaining the earlier observation for 1.240 s would be safe or necessary. Twenty-eight control episodes had already-published continuation. Replay odometry candidate gaps were 20 ms; observed command recorder p99/max in the multiwaypoint run were 20.508/40.510 ms, with a different wall clock and no adapter receipt. The nominal mission timer and command periods must be treated as configured intent, not measured worst-case callback bounds.

`PROPOSED_BOUND` remains symbolic: `T_retention_required ≥ crossing→matching-readiness delay + callback/transport reserve`, bounded above by route/localization identity validity, source-gap evidence, departure/re-entry policy and the maximum spatial travel that still makes the observation attributable to the same gate. A source-time and distance/route-cursor rule is required; an arbitrary 250/500 ms latch is not admitted. World revision alone does not erase a physical observation, but it can revoke its execution readiness. Reverse motion, odometry gap, route revision, localization reset, mission reset and accepted advance must invalidate or reclassify the observation.

`UNVERIFIED_MARGIN`: no distribution of crossing-first delays (N=1), no adapter receive/callback queue tail, no measured distance envelope for safe delayed consumption, and no self-intersection/reset runtime traces. Retention duration and spatial departure rule are **SPECIFICATION_GAP**. The current re-entry episode does not authorize consuming an old observation after leaving the ball. Existing model tests support identity/invalidation logic only; they are not a runtime retention qualification.
