# Destructive transition inventory before product mutation

Baseline is `49e9c0e8` only. The companion CSV lists **72** source call sites/owner operations with the requested trigger, asynchrony, desired/execution/causal witnesses, mutation, classification and repair. This inventory was completed before changing product code. It covers all RuntimeNode calls matching `failClosedLocked`, `ExecutionAuthority::{failClosedIfActiveSnapshot,failClosed,reset,clearGoal,invalidateIfCurrent,invalidate,clearRestartFromRest,suspendCommand}`, and `pending_goal_owner_.clearGoal`, plus owner-level conditional revocation and the world publication finalizer. There are **23 raw `failClosedLocked()` calls** plus two `failClosedIfActiveSnapshot()` calls; the private primitive definition is not counted as a caller.

Initial CSV categories: 15 `GLOBAL_SAFETY_EVENT`, 8 `LEGITIMATE_RESET`, 16 `EXACT_EXECUTION_OWNER`, 2 `EXACT_PENDING_OWNER`, 18 `EXTERNAL_AUTHORITY_EVENT`, and **13 `STALE_CALLBACK_RISK`**. Categories describe the primary owner/witness at each source site. Zero sites are classified `DESIRED_ONLY` because the new-intent exposure suspension is an explicit synchronous non-retainable goal transition, not a planner-result callback, and route/admission corruption is a current Core mission-integrity failure. Those policy claims remain subject to tests; an ordinary failed replacement solve cannot borrow them.

## Risks requiring repair

1. `classifyPlannerResult()` has a destructive default. `kOptimizationFailed` with a command and success/finished without a committed candidate can reach fail-close even though HG-023 retains a still-certified incumbent through failed replacement planning. The `RunCycle` fail-close branch checks desired revision and a captured active snapshot, but not the complete causal planning context at mutation. Unknown status must not silently gain revocation authority.
2. `RunCycle` PlanFromRest timeout captures the active snapshot and desired revision, but it can advance failure-window bookkeeping or report a timeout after the attempt/lifecycle changed. It needs the exact stopped-recovery state and attempt context at mutation.
3. The command publisher computes callback-start state freshness on lease L1 before the transition lock. A valid L2 can be published before the latch/fail-close. The final authorization failure can similarly be superseded between `publishIfCurrent` and the later transition lock. Both require the exact failed ingress lease at mutation; the second re-entry after cancellation cannot clear a newer pending goal.
4. The planner watchdog pins only solve generation/time. Desired intent and execution can advance before the watchdog takes the command transition lock; a timeout from the old solve may fail-close a newer active execution or clear its pending successor. It needs ephemeral solve provenance, not persistent execution authority.
5. World freshness suspension, motion-after-stop response and retained-command validation already have substantial owner checks but must be reviewed for causal world/state witnesses at the destructive boundary. World publication itself uses a timeline-version and expected-active/pending transaction and is classified exact-owner PASS.
6. `ExecutionAuthority::failClosedIfActiveSnapshot()` accepts an empty expected/current active record if their null pointers and lineage match. Conditional active revocation must reject empty tokens and return a distinguishable disposition.

## Existing legitimate scopes

Localization reset is an epoch-wide invalidation, including pending, measured state, command owner and mission inputs. A foreign mission or explicit non-ACTIVE PX4 mode status changes external authority. Monotonic ID exhaustion, fatal shared worker and execution-owner invariant violation are current global failures. Their source paths do not pretend a failed successor solve owns the predecessor. The retained-command validator enters its destructive branches only after comparing the captured execution snapshot, callback desired revision, and final emergency/suffix evidence; its causal state/world freshness is audited separately in the repair.

The historical HG-023 gate in `docs/safety/runtime_safety_current.md` and targeted archive line 3384 is controlling: keep a still-certified MAIN/BACKUP across failed replacement solves only while latest-world sweep, finite duration, exact goal/lease and anchor limits pass. Retain-command disposition must route through those checks; it is not unconditional retention. The 100 ms adapter lease, 200 ms state boundary, 500 ms freshness and planner deadlines are unchanged.
